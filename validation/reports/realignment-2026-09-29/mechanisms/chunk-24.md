## Discoverability convention

A mechanism is "discoverable" when a walk, a detector or a peer node can find it by shape. That means one of:

- a pool impulse shape (`poolImpulse` with a `shape`), readable over p2p through development-vessel's resolve;
- a registered resolver shape advertised via discovery;
- an activity row that Thompson can select;
- a concept in concept-db that the drafter recalls.

Boredom has almost no registered resolver shapes. It is a *consumer* daemon, and most of its behaviour is in-process in the 4,467-line `index.ts`. For kept in-process behaviour I name the shape or concept that should carry it.

Fossils go to `archive/fossils/boredom-vessel/<name>/` in the super-repo, as a code tombstone plus a pointer to the last commit. Units are archived by removing the unit from `scripts/substrate/units/` with the same pointer. Nothing is deleted without a tombstone.

## What recurs, and why (for the class ledger)

- **write-read-mismatch** is the boredom signature. The gap reader was wrong 4 times; the snapshot (#10) is the fifth instance. Every one hand-parses a resolve reply or a path. The durable fix is one typed client for `/v2/impulses/resolve` that unwraps `body` in one place, plus the constant-field detector from #10.
- **duplicate-by-mirror**: #4, #5 and #6 are declared copies of dev-vessel and goal-host logic ("mirrors", "same fleet-wide envelope"). Each was added because the shape it needed did not exist. Minting the three shapes (`admittedGaps`, `spendEnvelope`, a shared joiner) removes the reason to copy.
- **selection-learning**: #1's 85% pick share for a no-op tick is the degenerate-router finding again. The cheap tick's reward, not its value, decides the pick.
- **Autonomous commits made two of the fossils**: `84cd2f2` (#15) and `f28bc06` (#5, #6). A post-land check for "tracked build output" and "new function body ≥80% similar to an existing one in another vessel" would have caught both.
