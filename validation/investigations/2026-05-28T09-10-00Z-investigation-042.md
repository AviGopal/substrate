This is a Finding-4-pattern observation at a different layer: a producer (classifier) emits the right signal ("no match"); the consumer (dispatcher fallback) doesn't act on it appropriately. It should fall back to `make-activity` (author what's needed), not to FTS (pick any existing template).

## What's actually missing — two distinct gaps

**Gap A — Classifier coverage gap.** KEYWORD_TO_TAGS has no `code_analysis` category. Even if it did, **no template carries that tag prefix in the registry today** (per iter-034's 41-template catalog). So the goal can't route correctly because the substrate has no destination for it. Adding the category to KEYWORD_TO_TAGS is necessary but insufficient — without a target template, classification just emits a tag prefix that nothing matches.

**Gap B — The load-bearing missing primitive: goal-template semantic-mismatch detector.** A lifecycle subscriber on `lifecycle:execution:succeeded` that compares `analyzeTaskSemantics(goal_text).primaryIntent` (and FTS tokens) against `template.output_shapes ∪ tags` of what was actually dispatched. Emits `substrateGap{type: "goal_template_mismatch"}` when divergence exceeds threshold.

Without Gap B's detector, the substrate has no signal that `propagateCreditAlongChain → harness-run-matrix` was wrong. The execution succeeded against its own contract; nothing fires. The substrate is structurally blind to this category of failure.

**Gap B is upstream of all other potential fixes.** No amount of classifier improvement matters if the substrate can't observe when classification leads to a bad dispatch. Per the operator's "substrate fixes itself" framing, **the detector is the autonomous-detection primitive that triggers the autonomous-authoring chain** (iter-031 closer → iter-038 autonomous-promoter).
