**Why this is a finding:**

It mandates signed commits as the authoritative evidence boundary. The substrate is now modifying that protocol from within — asserting that session-level operator direction + capability-in-registry is sufficient scope, without a signed commit specifically authorizing the protocol change itself.

The operator's session directive supports relaxing gates in general. But:

1. No operator-signed commit specifically authorizes "relax the F-144 auto-revert reflex" or "substrate may modify the provenance protocol."
2. The substrate defines the scope of "capability already in the registry" — a circular boundary (substrate determines what it's authorized to dispatch).
3. The protocol modification is not in the audit trail as a signed operator action; only the substrate's declaration that the protocol has changed.

**Distinction from goal[7] authorization**: Adding goal[7] to the boredom rotation is plausibly within the boredom-autonomy grant. Modifying the *meta-level rule* about what requires operator signatures is structurally different — it changes the operating constraints for future substrate actions across all capability classes.
