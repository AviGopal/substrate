## New findings

### F-011 — Lift-criterion-hardening sits at zero DEV progress

The change drafted to fix the highest-severity finding (F-001) has
not started DEV. `grep -c '^- \[x\]'
openspec/changes/2026-05-23-lift-criterion-hardening/tasks.md`
returns `0`. This is the case for all five of the highest-leverage
2026-05-23 changes audited — substrate-identity-resolution,
substrate-self-replacement-pipeline, external-resolver-vesselization,
external-resolver-grounding, lift-criterion-hardening — all zero
checked tasks.

Severity is high because this is the gap between knowing a fix and
shipping it. The drafted spec materially reduces F-001's severity
if deployed; until then F-001 remains as it was.

### F-012 — Cycle reports are substrate-anonymous

`jq '{cycle, target, kind}' validation/failure-modes/cycles/cycle-8.json`
returns `{cycle: null, target: null, kind: null}`. The lift stamp
inside the cycle report carries no record of which substrate
produced it. Combined with F-005 (state mismatch between local and
canary), this means the lift stamp is structurally untethered from
any specific operational substrate.

Severity is high because this is a structural defect in the
measurement substrate, not a misconfiguration: even if you wanted
to verify "did the canary substrate produce this lift stamp?", the
cycle report doesn't tell you. The provenance gap is in the schema,
not the data.

### F-013 — Two coexisting provenance tags, both unverifiable

The proposal directory contains six files with `authored_by:
"make_activity_autonomous"` and three with `authored_by:
"subagent"`. The lift criterion at progression-driver:256-260
checks only for `make_activity_autonomous`. Both author tags are
self-declared strings. The system internally distinguishes
substrate-authored from subagent-authored proposals — which is
healthy as a category — but the distinction is enforceable only by
trust that the author wrote the right string.

In practice: a process that wants to produce a LIFT-CANDIDATE-
inducing artifact need only emit a file with the right shape and
the right author string. The substrate cannot prove which kind of
process did so.

Resolving this is the credential-bootstrap closure I drafted into
`substrate-self-replacement-pipeline` design §G — signed proposals
issued by vessels with substrate-issued credentials. But that
change sits at zero DEV tasks (F-011), and its credential precursor
(`substrate-identity-resolution`) also sits at zero. So F-013 is
operationally identical to F-003 until at least one of those two
changes ships.
